# Signalling Provisional & Negotiable Content

Confident prose gets believed — and machine-generated text is *relentlessly* confident, so it reads as settled, fact-checked, non-negotiable. When you actually mean a draft, a proposal, a thinking-out-loud, or a question for the room, you have to **signal provisionality explicitly**. This works at two levels: **structural** (a label or section marking the doc/part as open) and **tonal** (prose calibrated to the evidence). The hidden benefit of the structural move: one prominent "this is a draft" label *frees the prose to be direct* — you needn't hedge every sentence once you've declared the status once.

## Structural mechanisms

- **A status header** — `Status: Draft / In review / Proposed / Accepted`. (From Architecture Decision Records: keep *superseded* items with a forward reference rather than deleting — mark old thinking no-longer-operative without erasing it.)
- **An epistemic-status note** — a one-line top disclaimer of how much to trust what follows ("Epistemic status: speculative, low confidence"). Be clear which you mean: *do I still endorse this / how well-supported is it / how should you weight it.*
- **A "Request for Comments" / "for discussion" framing** — names the document non-authoritative and invites the response you want (the IETF RFC move).
- **A strawman** — a deliberately rough proposal put forward *to be attacked and improved* (the opposite of the strawman *fallacy*; it invites the strongest critique).
- **Dedicated open-content sections** — "Open Questions", "Decisions to make", "Alternatives considered" — quarantine the unsettled so it reads as unsettled.
- **Per-claim markers** — where a whole-doc label is too coarse: "(settled)", "(proposed)", "(open — needs data)", or a confidence note inline (the Toulmin qualifier made visible).

## Tonal calibration

Match the prose to the label: state what's firm firmly, hedge what's genuinely uncertain — and not the reverse. Two stances name the posture: **"strong opinions, weakly held"** (form a clear position to test, then seek what would overturn it) and **"disagree and commit"** (voiced disagreement before a decision is legitimate; commitment after doesn't require agreement). Avoid **reflexive hedging** — fogging every sentence so the reader can't tell what you know; aggregate the provisionality into one honest structural signal instead.

## Apply it

- **Declare the document's status up front** so nothing below is mistaken for settled.
- **Quarantine open material** in named sections.
- **Float rough ideas as explicit strawmen.**
- **Tag the claim, not just the document,** where confidence varies within a piece.
- **For AI-assisted drafts especially:** the model won't add the provisionality signal — add it back yourself.

## Failure modes

- **Unmarked draft** actioned as a decision.
- **Confidence-tone mismatch** — certain-sounding prose over loosely-held claims (the default failure of generated text).
- **Reflexive hedging** — every sentence fogged.
- **Buried openness** — open questions scattered instead of collected.
- **Delete-don't-supersede** — erasing prior thinking instead of marking it superseded.
