# White Papers

A B2B white paper is an **authoritative, educational-but-persuasive** document — it educates a business reader about an issue and, *by educating*, persuades them toward a viewpoint or purchase. It is gated to generate leads, builds thought-leadership authority, and supports a considered decision. The cardinal rule follows from the hybrid: **the body must be genuinely educational, not a sales pitch** — "making it look like a sales piece is the kiss of death" (Gordon Graham). A capable writer usually leads problem-first and brings evidence; the moves they skip are the **abstract**, the **type choice**, and **position-don't-pitch**.

## The recipe

1. **Pick the type** (from the reader's stage):
   - **Backgrounder** — explains a technology/method in depth; for a reader already evaluating.
   - **Problem/solution** — argues from a felt business problem, through evidence, to a recommended approach; the default lead-generator, for a reader who has the problem but not the solution.
   - **Numbered list** — "5 things to know about X"; light, scannable, shareable.
2. **Structure** (~6–8 pages body, ~10–12 total): Title (benefit/problem-oriented) → **Abstract / executive summary (~200 words, stands alone)** → Introduction (the problem, why it matters, scope) → Body (develop the problem, marshal evidence, build to the solution) → Conclusion/recommendation → **Call to action** → short About-the-company → References.
3. **Hold the educational-persuasion balance.** Lead with the reader's problem, not your product. Argue with evidence (data, references) — this is the authority the format trades on. **Position, don't pitch**: the solution can favour your approach, but as the reasoned conclusion of the analysis; keep overt selling to the CTA and boilerplate.

## Required moves

A strong draft usually opens problem-first with good evidence, then skips these. Check each:

- [ ] **A stand-alone abstract / executive summary up front** (~200 words) — many read only this; it must carry the argument by itself.
- [ ] **Type chosen deliberately** for the reader's stage (problem/solution is the default).
- [ ] **Problem-first opening, in the reader's terms** — not your product or company.
- [ ] **Every claim evidenced** — data, references, reasoned analysis. Unsupported assertion reads as marketing and the authority collapses.
- [ ] **Position by evidence, don't pitch.** Discriminators made visible *by the analysis*; the overt sell confined to the CTA.
- [ ] **A clear call to action** at the end, and **short** boilerplate (a long "about us" reframes the whole thing as marketing).

## Failure modes

- **The brochure in disguise** — a sales pitch dressed as a white paper; the kiss of death.
- **Assertion without evidence** — claims with no data or references.
- **Product-first opening** — leading with your solution before the reader feels the problem.
- **No stand-alone abstract** — the most-read part missing or unable to stand alone.
- **Wrong type for the stage**, or **bloated boilerplate**.

## Neighbours (don't confuse)

- vs **blog post**: white paper is long, authoritative, infrequent, lead-gen; blog is short, conversational, frequent, discovery-driven.
- vs **case study**: a case study tells *one customer's* story; a white paper addresses the *issue*.
- vs **hands-on handbook**: a handbook is a complete how-to reference; a white paper is a shorter, sharper persuasive argument. (For a practitioner "do-this-now" document, use `field-guide.md`.)
