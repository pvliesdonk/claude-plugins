# Writing Non-Fiction

Distilled craft for writing that informs, argues, and persuades. Each genre has its own reference file with a recipe — the structure, the moves writers skip, the failure modes. **Identify the genre, load its file, follow it.** Don't draft from the router alone.

## Core moves (every genre)

Apply these before any genre-specific step. Capable writers — humans and models — reliably do **1–2** well and **skip 3–6**. Check each one explicitly:

1. **Name the real reader, then write to them.** A specific person with a specific question — not "the audience." Beat the curse of knowledge: define or cut every term of art they don't already share.
2. **Front-load the point.** Lead with the conclusion / proposition / finding. The reader decides in the first lines whether to continue; never bury the bottom line.
3. **State the one-sentence spine.** "We know X *and* Y, *but* Z, *therefore* W." If that sentence isn't compelling, the document won't be. Build outward from it.
4. **Differentiate against the alternatives.** Name what the reader would otherwise do — the status quo, a competitor, the obvious method — and why your line beats it. Generic merit persuades no one against an unnamed rival.
5. **Evidence claims; don't assert them.** Replace "deep expertise / clearly / proven / robust" with a proof point, a citation, a number, a named example. Asserted authority reads as weak.
6. **Revise for skimming.** Make each section's first sentence carry its point; short sentences; plain words. Generate the draft first, then tune.

Then open the genre file and follow its recipe.

## Underlying techniques (tools)

The core moves and genre recipes draw on cross-cutting craft tools — front-loading, plain language, sentence clarity, argument structure, the gap (CARS), narrative spines, audience analysis, the Heilmeier catechism, AI-tells, revision, figures, and signalling provisional/negotiable content (so a draft reads as open, not settled). When drafting or (especially) revising and you want the technique itself, see `tools.md` for the index and the matching `tool-*.md` file.
