# Sentence Clarity

When sentences are correct but clunky, three complementary tools fix them — working *below* word choice, at cohesion, emphasis, and structure.

## Gopen & Swan — reader expectations (positions of power)
Readers expect certain information in certain places; honour the expectations:
- **Topic position (sentence start):** old / familiar information — what links back to what came before. Start from what the reader already knows.
- **Stress position (sentence end):** new / important information — the payload you want emphasised. Put it last.
- **Old-to-new flow** across sentences builds cohesion: a paragraph reads as connected when each sentence starts from the previous one's end.

## Williams — characters as subjects, actions as verbs
- Make the **main characters the grammatical subjects** and **their actions the verbs.** "The committee decided" beats "a decision was reached by the committee".
- **Un-bury nominalizations** — actions frozen into abstract nouns: "conduct an investigation of" → "investigate".

## Lanham — the Paramedic Method (cut the lard)
Circle the prepositions; find the real action; make it a strong verb; start fast (cut the windup). The **Lard Factor** is how much you cut without losing meaning — often 30–50%.

## Apply it
1. Who's doing what? Make them the subject and verb (Williams).
2. Does each sentence start old and end new? (Gopen & Swan).
3. Cut the lard — prepositional chains, "there is/are", windups (Lanham).

## Failure modes
- **Nominalization fog** — actions hidden in nouns, agents missing.
- **Payload in the wrong place** — the important phrase mid-sentence, lost.
- **Passive by default** — agent dropped, energy gone.
