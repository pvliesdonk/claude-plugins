# Front-Loading the Conclusion

One idea in four hats: **BLUF** (bottom line up front), the **inverted pyramid** (journalism), **SCQA** (consulting), and **answer-first / point-first** (Minto) all say the same thing — **lead with the conclusion, then support it.** A reader decides in the first lines whether to continue and often reads no further, so the most important content must come first.

## The move
- **State the bottom line in the first sentence or paragraph** — the decision, the finding, the recommendation, the ask. Not the background, not the journey to it.
- **Then support, in descending order of importance** — a reader who stops anywhere still has the most important part.
- **Each section and paragraph front-loads too** — its first sentence carries its point, so the document is scannable by reading first sentences alone.
- **SCQA opener** when context is needed: Situation (an agreed fact) → Complication (what changed / why now) → Question (often implied) → **Answer** (your point) — and the Answer still leads the body.

## The one exception
A deliberate **feature/narrative lead** (an anecdote that earns attention) may precede the point — but the "nut graf" (here's the point and why it matters) must arrive fast.

## Apply it
- Find the single most important sentence in the draft. Is it first? If not, move it there.
- Cut the windup ("This document discusses…", "In order to understand X, we must first…").
- Read only the first sentence of each paragraph — do they tell the story on their own?

## Failure modes
- **Buried lede** — the conclusion in the last paragraph, after the reasoning.
- **Throat-clearing** — background before the point.
- **Chronology over importance** — told in the order it happened, not the order that matters.
