# AI Tells of Prose Synthesis

Machine-drafted prose has a recognisable register. Editing it out matters because it reads as low-effort and quietly drains credibility — and because the tells are *fillers*, so cutting them improves the prose regardless of who wrote it.

## The tells to hunt
- **Empty openers / register-raisers:** "In today's fast-paced / rapidly evolving world / landscape…", "It is important to note that…", "It is crucial / essential to understand…".
- **Hollow abstractions & buzzwords:** "leverage", "holistic approach", "navigate the complexities", "foster a culture of", "in the realm of", "a testament to", "underscores", "delve".
- **Hedged sameness:** every sentence the same medium length and shape; relentless evenness, no rhythm, no short punchy lines.
- **Summary throat-clearing:** "Ultimately,", "In conclusion,", "At the end of the day," restating what was just said.
- **Compulsive triples:** the rule of three everywhere ("clear, concise, and compelling").
- **Vague booster adjectives:** "robust", "seamless", "comprehensive", "cutting-edge", "ever-changing".
- **No specifics:** claims with no number, name, or concrete example — the deepest tell.

## Apply it
- Delete the openers and the closers; start on the actual point.
- Replace each abstraction with the concrete thing it gestures at — or cut it.
- Vary sentence length deliberately; add a short one.
- Add a real specific (a number, a name, an example) wherever the prose floats.
- Read aloud: where it sounds like a press release, rewrite in your own voice.

## Failure modes
- **Polishing the tells instead of cutting them** — they're fillers; remove, don't rephrase.
- **Specifics still missing** after the edit — vagueness is the tell that survives a light pass.
