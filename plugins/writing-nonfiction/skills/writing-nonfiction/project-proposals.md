# Project Proposals & Plans (internal)

Pitching a project to your **own organisation** — a migration, a new product line, a process change, a research initiative — then planning it. The reader is an internal **sponsor** weighing this against everything else the org could spend the same money and people on. Two consequences: you compete against **other internal priorities and the status quo**, and you can lean on the shared language of project management. Two halves: **justify it** (the business case) and **plan it**.

## The recipe

1. **Business case — against a "do nothing" baseline.** The problem/opportunity; an **options appraisal that explicitly includes doing nothing** (compare *do nothing / do minimum / do this*, with do-nothing as the baseline the others beat); benefits **and dis-benefits**, with how they're measured; costs, timescale, major risks; a recommendation (+ ROI/payback for a money decision). Front-load all of it.
2. **Charter — get it authorised.** The short document that *authorises* the project and grants spend authority: objectives, scope (in **and** out), stakeholders, high-level milestones/budget/risks, success criteria, sponsor sign-off, link to a strategic goal. The charter authorises; it is **not** the detailed plan.
3. **Plan — scope → work → schedule → people → risk.** Scope statement (incl. exclusions) → **WBS** (deliverable-oriented decomposition; the foundation cost/schedule/risk build on) → schedule/milestones (Gantt) → **RACI** (one **A**ccountable per task) → budget → **risk register** (each risk: likelihood, impact, named owner, mitigation).
4. **Define success + de-risk.** Measurable success criteria agreed **up front** (SMART). Run a **pre-mortem**: imagine it has *already failed*, work backward to causes — surfaces risks a "what could go wrong?" round misses, and lets doubters speak safely. Feed results into the risk register.
5. **One-pager for buy-in.** Compress to a page: lead with the recommendation and the ask → 3–5 proof points (benefit, cost, timescale, the do-nothing comparison) → the explicit decision you need. The sponsor may read only this.

## Required moves

A strong draft usually describes the project and a plausible plan, then skips these. Check each:

- [ ] **A "do nothing" baseline.** Show why the status quo is worse than acting — the internal status-quo competitor. (Most-skipped.)
- [ ] **Options, not just your preferred one.** Do-minimum and do-this compared, not a single take-it-or-leave-it.
- [ ] **Charter and plan kept distinct.** Authorisation first; detailed plan second — don't conflate or skip the authorisation.
- [ ] **WBS before schedule.** Decompose *what* (deliverables) before scheduling *when*.
- [ ] **RACI with exactly one Accountable** per task (not zero, not two).
- [ ] **Measurable success criteria, up front** (SMART) — so "done" and "worked" aren't argued later.
- [ ] **Named risks + a pre-mortem** — a risk-free plan reads as naïve.
- [ ] **Lead the one-pager with the ask**, for a sponsor who reads only the top.

## Failure modes

- **No "do nothing" baseline** — benefits with nothing to weigh them against.
- **Selling against no alternative** — ignoring competing priorities and the status quo.
- **Charter-as-plan / plan-as-charter** — authorisation and detailed planning conflated.
- **WBS confused with the schedule** — *when* listed before *what* is decomposed.
- **Broken RACI** — two accountable owners, or none.
- **No measurable success criteria; a risk-free plan** — no register, no pre-mortem.

## Notes & neighbours

- **SMART** = Specific, Measurable, Assignable, Realistic, Time-related (Doran's original 1981 terms; the common "Achievable/Relevant/Time-bound" is a later reinterpretation).
- **Pre-mortem** = Gary Klein (*Sources of Power*, 1998; HBR 2007).
- vs the **external** siblings: a buyer (`contract-proposals.md`) or a funder panel (`research-grants.md`) instead of an internal sponsor; the internal case turns on options-vs-do-nothing and the PM artefacts.
