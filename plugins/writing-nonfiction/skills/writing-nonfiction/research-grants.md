# Research-Grant Proposals

A proposal asking a **funder** — a panel of reviewers scoring against a published rubric — to pay for research that doesn't exist yet. Close kin to a contract-research pitch, but the reader is a busy, often **non-specialist** panellist scoring criterion by criterion, not a company buyer. The controlling document is always the specific **call text**; templates, page limits, and criteria change between programmes.

## The reader: a panel scoring a rubric

A reviewer reads many proposals fast and is frequently not in your sub-field. So: **clarity and a graspable central idea win** (a proposal that assumes specialist knowledge underscores on significance), and you must **write to the rubric, in its language, in its order** — reviewers score section by section.

## The recipe

1. **Pick the scheme; read its rules first.** The shape differs fundamentally:
   - **Horizon Europe (collaborative RIA/IA)** — scored on **Excellence · Impact · Implementation**; a Part B section per criterion; **Impact is a full, equally-weighted section.**
   - **ERC (frontier)** — excellence-only, judged on the *idea* and the *PI*; B1 (idea + CV) then B2 (how); **no preliminary data required.**
   - **NIH/NSF (US)** — five criteria; the one-page Specific Aims; **preliminary data expected.**
   Don't import one scheme's shape into another's template.
2. **Pin the central idea** (jargon-free). The Heilmeier brief: objective, what's new, why it'll work, who cares, risks, cost, time, success checks. One ABT sentence: "We know X *and* Y, *but* Z is unsolved, *therefore* we'll do W." If that sentence isn't compelling, the proposal won't be.
3. **Justify the gap.** Establish the territory (what's known) → the **niche** (the specific gap — the move applicants most under-state) → occupy it (your aims). A *synthesised* literature review, not a list. Reviewers fund gaps that matter, not topics.
4. **Plan a credible approach, and name the risks.** Objectives → work packages → methods → milestones/deliverables → timeline. **Name the real risks and mitigations** — a risk-free plan reads as naïve. Supply feasibility evidence (preliminary data for NIH/NSF; not for ERC).
5. **Make the impact case** — load-bearing, and where Horizon proposals most often fail.
6. **Team, budget, timeline; write to the criteria; revise.** Right people for *this* work, evidenced. Open each section in the rubric's language; front-load each section's point. If rejected, most schemes allow one resubmission answering each critique point-by-point.

## Required moves

A strong draft usually states a clear central idea and a plausible impact story, then **skips the rest**. Check each:

- [ ] **Central idea, jargon-free**, graspable by a non-specialist panellist. (Usually done.)
- [ ] **A specific, motivated niche** — not "more research is needed." Name precisely what's missing and why it matters.
- [ ] **Write to the named criteria.** Structure and open each section in the rubric's own words and order, so the panel can score it line by line.
- [ ] **Impact as a real section, not a flourish.** Name the three pathways explicitly — **Dissemination** (results to community/industry/policy), **Exploitation** (further development/commercialisation/application), **Communication** (public engagement) — and **quantify** expected outcomes (targets, KPIs, who benefits and how much). "A step change" is not an impact case.
- [ ] **Name the risks and mitigations.** The real ones, with contingencies.
- [ ] **Feasibility evidence appropriate to the scheme** (preliminary data for NIH/NSF; track record + bold idea for ERC).
- [ ] **Right scheme shape.** Don't paste a Specific-Aims page into a Horizon template.

## Failure modes

- **Writing for your sub-field** — specialist assumptions a generalist panel can't follow.
- **A weak gap** — a topic, not a motivated niche.
- **A thin Impact section** — the most common Horizon failure; impact treated as an afterthought, un-pathwayed, unquantified.
- **Ignoring the rubric** — not addressing a criterion explicitly, so it can't be scored.
- **A risk-free plan** — reads as naïve.
- **Wrong scheme shape** — one funder's structure forced into another's template.
