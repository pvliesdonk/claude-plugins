# Narrative Spines (ABT & OCAR)

Two tools to give non-fiction a story spine, so an argument *moves* instead of listing.

## ABT — And, But, Therefore (the one-sentence engine)
Reduce the whole thing to one sentence: "**We know X *and* Y, *but* Z (the problem/tension), *therefore* W (what we do or found).**" The *and* sets agreed context; the *but* introduces the tension that makes it interesting; the *therefore* delivers the consequence. **The *but* is the load-bearing word** — no tension, no story. If you can't fill the ABT compellingly, you don't yet have the spine.

## OCAR — the longer arc
For a whole document: **O**pening (context) → **C**hallenge (the question/problem) → **A**ction (what was done) → **R**esolution (what it means). Point-first variants (open in the action) suit impatient readers. OCAR is the *principle*; the genre template (IMRaD, the proposal structure) is the *implementation*.

## Apply it
- Write the ABT before drafting. Is there a real *but*? A list of true facts with no tension is not an argument.
- Map the document to OCAR: is there a challenge and a resolution, or just an opening and a data dump?

## Failure modes
- **AAA, no BT** — "and… and… and…": facts with no tension or consequence.
- **No resolution** — action reported, meaning never landed.
