# The Gap (CARS)

How to justify why a piece of work matters — Swales's "Create A Research Space" model, the move-structure of a strong introduction and of any "why this, why now" case.

## The three moves
1. **Establish the territory** — what's known, what matters in this area. Briefly; the reader needs the lay of the land.
2. **Establish the niche — the gap.** What's missing, unsolved, contradictory, or unexamined. This is the move writers most often under-state or skip, and it's the one that *justifies the work*. Name it specifically: not "little is known", but exactly what isn't known and why that's a problem.
3. **Occupy the niche** — your contribution: what you do to fill the gap, previewing the aims and structure.

## Apply it
- State the gap in one specific sentence: "Despite X, no one has Y, which matters because Z."
- Check the gap is *motivated* — does filling it matter, or is it merely unfilled? Readers (and reviewers) care about gaps that matter, not novelty for its own sake.
- Check Move 3 actually fills the gap named in Move 2 — they must match.

## Failure modes
- **No niche** — a literature summary that never says what's missing.
- **A weak gap** — "more research is needed" instead of a specific, motivated hole.
- **Mismatch** — the contribution doesn't fill the gap you claimed.
