# Plain Language

Clarity at the word and sentence surface: write so the reader understands on the **first** reading. The plain-language movement (and ISO 24495-1) defines it that way — the reader can find what they need, understand it, and use it, first time. Readability formulas (Flesch, Fog) measure a *proxy* (word and sentence length); use them as a smoke alarm, not a target to game.

## The moves
- **Short, common words over long, fancy ones.** "use" not "utilize"; "about" not "with regard to"; "buy" not "purchase".
- **Short sentences, one idea each.** Break a 40-word sentence into two.
- **Cut deadweight.** "in order to" → "to"; "due to the fact that" → "because"; "at this point in time" → "now".
- **Define or banish jargon.** Every term of art is a barrier — define it on first use or replace it.
- **Active voice, real subjects.** "We decided" not "a decision was made".
- **Concrete over abstract.** Name the thing, the person, the number.

## Apply it
- Read it aloud; where you stumble or run out of breath, cut or split.
- Hunt nominalizations (verbs frozen into nouns: "make a decision" → "decide"; "provide assistance" → "help").
- Treat a readability score as a check, not a goal: a bad score flags a problem; a good score doesn't prove clarity.

## Failure modes
- **Jargon as credential** — field terms used to sound expert; reads as "can't explain it".
- **Bureaucratic register** — "pursuant to", "said process", "effectuate", "utilize".
- **Gaming the formula** — chopping prose into choppy fragments to hit a grade level.
