# The Heilmeier Catechism

Eight questions (George Heilmeier, DARPA) that pressure-test any proposal, project, or idea. Answer all eight **in plain language, no jargon** — the discipline is as much about clarity as completeness. A question you can't answer cleanly is a hole the reader will find.

1. **What are you trying to do?** State the objective with no jargon.
2. **How is it done today, and what are the limits** of current practice?
3. **What's new in your approach, and why will it succeed?**
4. **Who cares?** If you succeed, **what difference does it make?**
5. **What are the risks?**
6. **How much will it cost?**
7. **How long will it take?**
8. **What are the mid-term and final "exams"** to check for success — the concrete checkpoints at which progress can be judged?

## Apply it
- Use it as a *pre-writing brief*: answer the eight before drafting; the answers become raw material (Q1/Q3 → the core idea; Q4 → the value/significance; Q5–Q8 → the plan and de-risking).
- Q1's "no jargon" is the test — if you can't state the objective so a smart non-specialist gets it, the idea isn't clear enough yet.
- Run it as a **red-team**: have a skeptical colleague ask each question of your draft and point to *where it's answered* (not explain it aloud).

## Failure modes
- **No "who cares"** — a capable plan no one needs.
- **No exams** — no way to tell whether it's working.
- **Jargon answers** — hiding an unclear idea behind terms of art.
