# Blog Posts

A blog post is read on a screen by someone who **scans rather than reads**, arrived from a search or a link, and decides in a second or two whether to stay. (Nielsen Norman research: ~79% of users scan; they read a fraction of the words.) The craft follows from that reader: earn the **click** with the headline, the **stay** with the first line, the **read** by being scannable and front-loaded. A capable writer usually handles scannability and hook well; the moves they still skip are **one-idea discipline** and a **real (non-AI-generic) voice**.

## The recipe

1. **Headline — earn the click.** Most people read only the headline. Make it **specific and clear over clever** (the reader should know what they'll get) and **honest** (clickbait the post doesn't pay off destroys trust and ranking).
2. **First line — earn the stay.** The opening sentence's only job is to earn the second: a sharp claim, a concrete image, a real question — never "In today's fast-paced world…".
3. **Front-load the point.** Inverted pyramid for the web: conclusion early, then develop. A scanner who stops after two paragraphs should still have your point. (Exception: an anecdotal feature lead, but the nut graf — "here's why it matters" — must come fast.)
4. **Make it scannable.** Short paragraphs, one idea each (the first sentence carries the paragraph); meaningful subheadings (navigable by scanning headings alone); bulleted lists; bold key phrases, sparingly; plain, objective language.
5. **One idea, real voice, a next step.** One core takeaway; a conversational, authentic voice; end with a question, a related read, or a call to act.

## Required moves

A strong draft usually nails the hook and scannability, then slips on these. Check each:

- [ ] **Specific, honest headline** — concrete value, no clickbait. (Spend on it; it's most of the job.)
- [ ] **A hook, not a windup** — the first line earns the second.
- [ ] **Point front-loaded** — value before the scanner leaves.
- [ ] **Scannable** — short paragraphs (first sentence carries each), real subheadings, lists, sparing bold, plain words.
- [ ] **One idea, ruthlessly.** Cut everything that doesn't serve the single takeaway; an everything-post is remembered for nothing.
- [ ] **A real voice** — a person talking, not a corporate/AI-generic register. Strip synthetic-sounding prose (the over-used register-words, the hedged sameness); the voice is the differentiator.
- [ ] **A next step / CTA** at the end.

## Failure modes

- **The slow windup** — value buried behind throat-clearing; the scanner is gone.
- **The wall of text** — long unbroken paragraphs; nothing for the scan to grab.
- **Clickbait headline** — a promise the post doesn't keep.
- **The everything-post** — no single idea.
- **The corporate/AI voice** — generic, hedged, register-raised, no person behind it.

## A note on SEO (light, fast-changing)

Write for people, not search engines (Google's "helpful content" / E-E-A-T consensus). A descriptive title and meta description, sensible headings, search-intent fit, and links help; keyword-stuffing hurts. Apply light SEO *after* writing for the reader. (Tactics change with every algorithm update — treat specifics as perishable.)
