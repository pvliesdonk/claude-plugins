# Convergence & Deciding

Narrowing many options to a committed few is the harder, less-loved half. Done crudely ("everyone okay with this?") it manufactures false agreement that collapses in execution. Three layers: **gauge** where people stand, **sort** the list, **decide** by an agreed rule.

## Gauge (read the room honestly)
- **Gradients of agreement** (Kaner) — replace yes/no with a scale: endorse / agree with reservations / mixed / stand aside / block. **Poll silently first**, then discuss to a pre-agreed threshold. Surfaces quiet reservations before they sink the decision later.
- **Fist to Five** — fast 0–5 show of hands; coarser and public (prone to herding).

## Sort (cut the list)
- **Dot voting** — fixed dots per person across options. Watch the pitfalls: herding (vote privately first), vote-splitting (cluster near-duplicates first), and breadth-vs-intensity (dots can't tell ten mild from two passionate).
- **Multivoting (N/3)** — each person gets ~⅓ of the options as votes, marked privately; drop the bottom and repeat. Surfaces broadly-acceptable options.

## Prioritise (rank what's left)
- **Impact–Effort** — 2×2: quick wins / big bets / fill-ins / time sinks. Rate *independently* first; the split ratings are the productive discussions.
- **MoSCoW** — Must / Should / Could / **Won't-this-time**. The discipline is forcing the Won't list, or everything inflates to Must.

## Decide by a rule — agreed *first*
- The decision-rule spectrum trades speed for commitment: leader-decides → leader-with-input → majority → **consent** → consensus. State which *before* deciding, or half the room thinks they decided and half think they advised.
- **Consensus** ("does everyone agree it's best?") vs. **consent** (sociocracy: "any reasoned, paramount objection?"). Consent is faster — it clears "no one has a principled problem," not "everyone prefers this"; a mere preference doesn't block.
- **Close on owned action** — one named owner (never "the team") + a date per next step.

## Failure modes
- **False consensus** — silence read as agreement; sinks at execution.
- **Public-vote herding / vote-splitting** — sequential visible votes; similar options dividing support.
- **Unstated decision rule** — no real authority; the decision unravels.
- **Must-have inflation** — no Won't list.
