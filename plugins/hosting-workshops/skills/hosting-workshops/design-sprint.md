# Running a Design Sprint

The most tightly-scripted workshop format: five days taking a wicked problem from question to tested prototype (Knapp / Google Ventures). It's heavyweight — up to seven people for up to five days — so the first job is confirming it's the right tool; the rest is disciplined facilitation of a fixed structure. The moves people skip: pre-assigning the Decider, holding the silent-individual rhythm, and actually testing.

## The recipe

1. **Qualify hard.** Only the full sprint for a *high-stakes, wicked* problem a prototype+test will resolve. Otherwise borrow the moves (`choosing.md`).
2. **Prep the team and roles.** ≤7, cross-functional. **Pre-assign the Decider** (real authority, attends Mon & Wed) — this is the decision rule, settled up front. The **Facilitator is not the Decider** (process vs content). Recruit Friday's test users; set up a room for the week.
3. **The five days:**
   - **Mon — Map:** long-term goal; map; expert interviews; *How Might We* notes; pick one target.
   - **Tue — Sketch:** lightning demos; **Crazy 8s**; a detailed, anonymous solution sketch (silent, individual).
   - **Wed — Decide:** art-museum + silent **heat-map** vote; speed critique; the Decider's binding **sticky decision**; storyboard.
   - **Thu — Prototype:** a realistic *facade* ("fake it," good-enough), 3pm trial run.
   - **Fri — Test:** five 1:1 user interviews; team observes; debrief for *patterns*; Decider sets next steps.
4. **Hold the structure.** Protect timeboxes and the *silent-individual-then-share* rhythm — the discipline *is* the method (it's the production-blocking fix and the Double Diamond on a calendar).

## Required moves

A strong sprint runs the activities and skips the structure. Check each:

- [ ] **Qualified** — wicked, high-stakes; otherwise borrow moves, don't run the week.
- [ ] **Decider pre-assigned and present; Facilitator ≠ Decider.**
- [ ] **Tuesday generation silent and individual** — no group brainstorming.
- [ ] **Wednesday vote silent-first; the Decider breaks ties** openly.
- [ ] **A "good-enough" facade, then a real five-user test** — not over-polished, not skipped.
- [ ] **If compressed, keep Decider + silent generation + a real test** — and don't call a half-day a sprint.

## Failure modes

- **Sprinting a routine problem** — five days for what a decision would settle.
- **No real Decider** — Wednesday's decision doesn't stick.
- **Group brainstorming on Tuesday** — production blocking returns.
- **An over-polished prototype** — no time to test, or users react to polish.
- **Skipping Friday** — deciding without testing; the point of the week, discarded.
