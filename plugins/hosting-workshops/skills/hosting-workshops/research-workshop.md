# Organizing a Research Workshop

Convening an academic / scientific workshop is a months-long *organising* job — most of what decides its quality happens before anyone's in the room. It's the convening role, not in-room facilitation (though that still matters on the day). The moves people skip: declaring archival status, running a real review process, and facilitating the day instead of chairing a talk-marathon.

## The recipe

1. **Choose the format and archival status — now.** Standard co-located **workshop** (CFP + light review) to incubate a topic; a **Dagstuhl-style residential** (no fixed program, no required talks) for deep expert synthesis; an **unconference / BoF** (Open Space lines) for participant-driven. Decide **archival vs non-archival** — it shapes who submits and whether they can publish the work elsewhere later. State it in the CFP.
2. **Work the ~9–15-month timeline.** Propose + secure co-location → publicise the CFP (4–6 mo before deadline) → review (1–2 mo) → camera-ready (~3 wk before) → run → wrap.
3. **Scope the CFP deliberately** — it's the design instrument; not so broad it's incoherent, not so narrow nothing comes in. Specify submission types, dates, format, archival status.
4. **Small, scope-covering PC; default double-blind.** A handful of the right reviewers (bigger correlates with lower quality); rotate yearly; enforce COI; 2–3 reviews/paper. Single-blind measurably favours prestige (Tomkins 2017) — default to double-blind.
5. **Facilitate the day, don't chair talks.** A workshop's value is community and discussion, not paper-presentation. Design an arc with discussion, working groups, and synthesis; use microstructures (`tool-methods.md`) so everyone engages, not just presenters; apply in-room safety and questioning.
6. **Publish the harvest** — proceedings (CEUR / ACM / IEEE / Dagstuhl series) and/or a synthesis report; debrief with co-organizers.

## Required moves

- [ ] **Format and archival status chosen up front** and stated in the CFP.
- [ ] **~12-month timeline worked** — propose/co-locate early.
- [ ] **CFP scoped deliberately; PC small; double-blind default; COI enforced.**
- [ ] **The day facilitated for discussion** — not back-to-back talks.
- [ ] **Harvest published** — proceedings and/or synthesis; debrief.

## Failure modes

- **The mini-conference** — back-to-back talks, no discussion. The signature failure.
- **A vague CFP** — wrong (or no) community submits.
- **Silent archival status** — authors can't judge whether submitting forecloses a journal version.
- **Single-blind by default** — the documented prestige bias, for no reason.
- **No synthesis** — a good day with no captured directions; the community benefit lost.
- **Too-late start** — rushed review and logistics.
