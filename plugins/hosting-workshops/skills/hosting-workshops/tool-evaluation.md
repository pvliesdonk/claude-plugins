# Evaluation & Follow-Through

Telling whether a workshop worked — and making it stick. The trap: the easiest thing to measure (did people enjoy it?) is the least connected to whether it made a difference. And the value mostly lands *after* the room empties.

## Measuring impact, honestly
- **Kirkpatrick's four levels** — Reaction → Learning → Behavior → Results — are a useful *checklist* (did you measure anything beyond satisfaction?). But the assumption that they form a **causal chain** is *not* supported: a good reaction barely predicts learning or behaviour (Alliger & Janak; Alliger et al.). Don't infer down the chain.
- **The smile-sheet problem** — end-of-session satisfaction surveys measure enjoyment, which correlates weakly with learning. If you must use a reaction survey, ask **utility / intent-to-apply** questions ("how likely are you to use this?"), not "did you enjoy it?" — and still don't treat it as proof.
- **Decide the evidence before the session** (backward design's Stage 2) — plan at least one learning/behaviour signal, not "we'll send a survey."

## The transfer problem (why follow-through matters)
Most of what's learned or decided in a session never reaches the work (Baldwin & Ford). (The famous "only 10% transfers" is a myth as a precise number — but transfer is reliably *far less* than assumed.) What helps: **manager/supervisor support** (the strongest lever), **application planning** (specific *if-then* intentions transfer; vague ones don't), and follow-up. Design for the environment and the plan, not just the day — the event is ~10%.

## Capture & close
- **The harvest in the room** — decisions and actions recorded with **one named owner + a date** each (never "the team"). A summary sent within ~24 h restores salience and gives managers something to reinforce.

## The after-action review (your own debrief)
Run a blame-free AAR with your co-facilitator right after, on four questions: **What was supposed to happen? What actually happened? Why the difference? What to sustain or improve?** Pair *sustain* with *improve*. It's the facilitator's own retrospective and the engine of getting better.

## Failure modes
- **Smile-sheet-only evaluation** — satisfaction declared success.
- **Assuming the causal chain** — "they loved it, so it worked."
- **Event-only design** — no application plan, manager support, or follow-up; it doesn't transfer.
- **The uncaptured harvest / "the team will own it"** — nothing owned, nothing acted on.
- **No AAR** — the same mistakes recur; good moves unnamed.
