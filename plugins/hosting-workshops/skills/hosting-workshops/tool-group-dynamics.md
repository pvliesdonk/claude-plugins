# Group Dynamics & Safety

A workshop is a temporary group under time pressure. Everything it needs — half-formed ideas, admitting confusion, challenging the senior person — is *interpersonal risk-taking*. Manage the climate, or you get silence and false consensus.

## Stages (Tuckman)
Groups move through **forming → storming → norming → performing** (+ adjourning). It's descriptive, not a law — groups skip, cycle, regress. For a one-day workshop you compress all of it into hours, so *deliberate openings* (speed forming) and *tolerating early friction* (storming — the same beast as the groan zone) matter.

## Psychological safety (the precondition)
A "shared belief the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking" (Edmondson) predicts whether people speak up. It is built in the **first minutes** — by your ground rules, your tone, and above all **how you receive the first risky contribution**. Shut one down and the room learns to stay quiet; receive it with genuine curiosity and it learns the opposite. Hierarchy is the biggest threat — a senior presence (or a leader who also facilitates) suppresses candor.

## A few ground rules (Schwarz)
Pick two or three and *model* them: **test assumptions**, **explain your reasoning**, **focus on interests, not positions**, **share relevant information**. Don't recite nine rules nobody follows.

## The hard moments — fix structurally, not personally
- **Dominators** — silent write-first generation, round-robins, small-group breakouts; make the *process* do the limiting so you don't police a person.
- **Quiet participants** — often the climate, not disengagement; the same write-first/breakout moves draw them out, plus asking the *group* and waiting.
- **Conflict** — treat as *data*, not disruption: surface the interests beneath the positions, make it discussable; don't smooth it over to keep things comfortable.

## Failure modes
- **Mistaking quiet for consensus** — the "decision" collapses afterward.
- **Suppressing storming** — disagreement goes underground, resurfaces worse.
- **Policing the dominator personally** — costs neutrality and safety.
- **Killing the first risky idea** — trains the whole room to stay quiet.
- **Ground-rule theatre** — posted rules nobody models.
