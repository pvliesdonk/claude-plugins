# Running a Retrospective

The most common recurring team workshop — and one of the most commonly hollowed-out, into either a blame session or a ritual nothing changes after. Done well it's a tight, safe, action-producing loop. The moves people skip: the safety opening, converging to a *few owned* actions, and checking last time's actions actually happened.

## The recipe

1. **Open with safety and the Prime Directive.** Read (or mean) Kerth's directive — "everyone did the best job they could, given what was known at the time…" — to reframe from *who screwed up* to *what to improve*. A retro lives or dies on `tool-group-dynamics.md` safety.
2. **Check last retro's actions first.** Did they happen? Visibly follow through, or the team stops believing this one's commitments matter either.
3. **Walk the five stages** (Derby & Larsen — really `tool-facilitation-moves.md` ORID applied to the team): **Set the Stage → Gather Data → Generate Insights → Decide What to Do → Close.** Gather facts *and* feelings before interpreting; ask "why" before solutioning.
4. **Converge to a few owned actions.** Dot-vote; pick **one or two** concrete changes with named owners and dates. One real change beats ten aspirations. This stage is non-negotiable.
5. **Rotate the format.** Start/Stop/Continue, 4Ls, Mad/Sad/Glad, Sailboat — vary it so the retro stays alive; match scaffolding to the team's maturity.
6. **Keep it short and regular.** ~60–90 min on a fixed cadence (per delivery cycle). Regular-and-brief beats occasional-and-marathon.

## Required moves

A strong retro gathers data well and skips the close and the safety. Check each:

- [ ] **Safety opened** (Prime Directive / check-in) before any data.
- [ ] **Last time's actions reviewed** at the top.
- [ ] **Five stages walked** — data and feelings before insights before actions; no early solutioning.
- [ ] **A few owned actions** (named owner + date), not a wish-list.
- [ ] **Format rotated**, scaffolding matched to the team.
- [ ] **Short and on a fixed cadence.**

## Failure modes

- **The blame session** — no safety; people defend instead of improve.
- **Insights without action** — ends at "what we learned," nothing owned. The commonest hollow retro.
- **Action amnesia** — last retro's commitments never checked, so no one believes this one's.
- **Same format forever** — the team goes through the motions.
- **The marathon retro** — rare and long instead of short and regular.
- **Solutioning too early** — jumping to fixes before the data and the "why."
