# Logistics & Production

The physical and operational layer — the part most hosts underprepare and most participants feel immediately. The room talks before you do.

## Room & seating (the arrangement is an instruction)
- **Rows** say "sit and receive" — kills lateral interaction; avoid for participatory work. **Circle / small rounds (4–6)** say "we're in this together" and are the workhorse for breakout and write-first work; a single circle signals equality.
- Two non-negotiables: **claimable wall space** (for affinity clustering — spread, move, step back) and **the ability to move** (breakouts; no facilitator "no-go" spots blocking sightlines). Walk the room before anyone arrives.

## Materials kit
Sticky notes that actually stick; **chisel-tip** markers (fine-point is unreadable across a room), one per person; flipchart paper + painter's tape; dot stickers; a visible timer; spares. Analog earns its place: simultaneous posting (no production blocking) and silent write-then-post as a leveller.

## Group size (the evidence is real)
Per-person contribution drops as groups grow (production blocking is worse in larger groups). **Break out by default**: pairs/triads for trust and airtime; **3–5** the workhorse; above ~7 needs a designated facilitator or it's dominated. Reserve plenary for framing, harvest, and witnessed decisions.

## Accessibility (ask proactively)
Put an accessibility line in the **invitation** — don't wait to be asked. Physical access (step-free, parking, bathrooms, cables, reachable outlets); sensory (PA/mic, lighting for lip-reading, captions); neurodiversity (quiet room, fragrance-free, high-contrast uncluttered slides, **clarity over cleverness**, discreet seating); clearly labelled dietary options; digital materials to WCAG.

## Contingency / Plan B & co-facilitation
- **Protect the start** (airtight first 2–3 h) and **the end, not the agenda** (know the one activity you'll drop).
- **Flex points** near lunch/end to absorb overruns; prepare ways to *shorten* (activities run long).
- **Run with a co-facilitator** — lead owns engagement and energy; co owns time, notes, materials, breakouts (and tech if hybrid). Agree non-verbal timing signals; don't change the agenda without consulting.

## Failure modes
- **Rows by default** — a participatory session set up like a lecture.
- **No wall / no breakout room** — the methods can't run; discovered on the day.
- **Plenary generation** — production blocking throttles it.
- **Reactive accessibility** — asking too late to arrange.
- **No Plan B / solo overload** — one failure derails it; one person can't engage *and* watch the clock *and* fix tech.
