# Strategy Offsite

The highest-stakes recurring leadership workshop, and the one most likely to be "offsite theatre" — energy, flip-charts, warm feelings, and nothing about the business changes after. The cause is almost always *insufficient structure*: false consensus and analysis instead of owned choices. The moves people skip are the decision discipline and the follow-through.

## The recipe

1. **Make a decision the objective.** "Decide our where-to-play for next year," not "align on strategy." Define the choices that will exist by the end (the evidence).
2. **Decide, don't analyse.** Run integrated *choices* (Lafley & Martin's cascade: winning aspiration → where to play → how to win → capabilities → systems). Refuse death-by-SWOT — a strength isn't a strength except relative to a choice. OKRs come *after*, to translate choices into owned goals.
3. **Bring a neutral facilitator.** The leader can't both run the room and decide in it — and their chairing suppresses candor (hierarchy is the biggest threat to safety). An external facilitator lets the leader argue *as a participant* and the room push back.
4. **Small senior room, pre-work done.** Few enough to converge; data and pre-reads sent ahead so the time is spent deciding, not briefing.
5. **Run a decision diamond** (`tool-the-arc.md`): diverge on the situation (surface real disagreement) → hold the groan zone (don't impose an answer) → converge on a few choices → close. Force genuine debate against groupthink: anonymous pre-poll, rotating devil's advocate, write-first breakouts, a *gradient of agreement* over "everyone okay?"
6. **Close for follow-through.** Owned commitments (named person + date), and a **90-day review cadence** with a first-two-weeks plan. Manager reinforcement is the strongest transfer lever; the cadence is what keeps strategy alive.

## Required moves

A strong offsite has good discussion and skips the structure. Check each:

- [ ] **Objective is a decision**, not vague "alignment."
- [ ] **Real choices made** (cascade), not SWOT quadrants filled.
- [ ] **A neutral facilitator** so the leader participates and candor survives.
- [ ] **Small room, pre-work circulated** — deciding, not briefing.
- [ ] **Genuine debate engineered** — pre-poll / devil's advocate / gradient, against false consensus.
- [ ] **Owners + dates + a 90-day cadence** — or it evaporates.

## Failure modes

- **Offsite theatre** — energy, no decision, nothing changes.
- **Death by SWOT** — lists instead of integrated choices.
- **Leader-as-facilitator** — suppressed candor, false consensus; can't both run and decide.
- **Vague "alignment" goal** — no decision to force.
- **The over-full room** — too many, too senior, can't converge.
- **No follow-through** — no owners, no cadence; the choices die.
