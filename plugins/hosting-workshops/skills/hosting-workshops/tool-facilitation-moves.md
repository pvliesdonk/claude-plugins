# In-Room Facilitation Moves

The moment-to-moment verbal craft: the question you ask next and how you listen to the answer. Done badly — leading questions, half-attention — it quietly tells the room this is theatre.

## ORID (the focused conversation)
Move a discussion through four levels, **in order**: **O**bjective (the facts — "what did we see?") → **R**eflective (reactions/feelings — "what surprised you?") → **I**nterpretive (meaning/patterns — "what does it mean?") → **D**ecisional (action — "what do we do?"). The classic mistake is jumping to Decisional before facts and reactions are shared, producing shallow decisions on unshared assumptions. ORID scales from a 5-minute debrief to a 2-hour conversation.

## Questions
- **Open vs. closed** — open ("what's driving that?") to generate and explore; closed ("do we agree?") to converge and lock. Premature closed questions kill divergence.
- **Genuine vs. leading** — a question that contains your preferred answer ("don't you think we should just…?") breaks neutrality; the group hands back your own view.

## Active listening
- **Paraphrase** ("so what I'm hearing is…") — checks understanding *and* signals the speaker was heard.
- **Summarise** faithfully — gather contributions into a shape the group can react to; reflect back in *their* terms, not your spin. A "summary" bent toward your preference is steering content.
- **Clarify** — ask for a specific example rather than assuming.

## Pacing, honestly
- **Wait after asking** — count to five in silence; the awkward pause is the thinking time quiet members need. Filling it trains the room to let you.
- **Vary the mode and read the room** — falling participation and shorter answers are real signals; the fixed "attention span" minute-count is a myth.

## Failure modes
- **Jumping to Decisional** — "so what should we do?" before facts and reactions.
- **Leading questions** — you learn nothing but your own view.
- **Listening to reply** — the speaker feels processed, not heard.
- **Spin-summarising** — neutrality lost.
- **Filling silence** — the quiet half never gets in.
