# Stakeholder / Requirements Workshop

A facilitated workshop reconciles multi-stakeholder requirements *live* — eliciting, surfacing conflict, and aligning in one room — instead of gathering one mental model at a time and reconciling later. It's the one elicitation method that can reach consensus in the session, and it lives or dies on facilitation. The move people skip: getting at the *need* under the stated *solution*.

## The recipe

1. **Map stakeholders first (Mendelow).** Plot power × interest; invite the **key players** (high/high) whose presence makes the output legitimate. Don't pad the room — too many can't converge.
2. **Use JAD roles.** A sponsor with decision authority; a **neutral** facilitator (no content); a scribe; SMEs who drive the content; the dev team as silent observers. Circulate a pre-brief in advance.
3. **Run the arc** (`tool-the-arc.md`): open → diverge on the situation → groan zone → converge → close.
4. **Elicit write-first** (`tool-ideation.md`). Silent-then-share stops senior stakeholders anchoring everyone. Then build shared understanding with **user story mapping** (backbone → stories → slices), use cases, and a **paper prototype / Product Box** to externalise latent priorities.
5. **Five-whys every solution.** Stakeholders state solutions ("must have a password"), not needs ("must be authenticated"). Peel back to the need — the defining move of the genre.
6. **Close on a prioritised, owned artifact.** **MoSCoW** (force the *Won't* list); sign off with a *gradient of agreement*, not "everyone okay?"; one owner + a date per action; a 24-hour summary.

## Required moves

A strong session nails the elicitation activities and skips the discipline. Check each:

- [ ] **Stakeholders mapped; key players in the room**, not a padded invite list.
- [ ] **Facilitator neutral; sponsor decides** — roles explicit; pre-brief sent.
- [ ] **Generation is write-first** — senior voices don't anchor the room.
- [ ] **Every stated solution five-why'd to its need.**
- [ ] **Prioritised with MoSCoW, Won't list forced** — not everything a Must.
- [ ] **Signed off with a gradient, not silence** — reservations surfaced now, not later as a change request.
- [ ] **Owners + dates captured; summary within 24h.**

## Failure modes

- **Solution bias** — capturing solutions as requirements instead of the needs beneath.
- **Dominant stakeholders** — fixed with write-first/breakouts, not by asking them to hold back.
- **Unstated assumptions** — divergent mental models never surfaced; force them with a model or prototype.
- **Scope creep** — use a stated non-scope and a visible parking lot.
- **Papered-over conflict** — smoothing competing requirements instead of surfacing and adjudicating with rationale.
- **False-consensus sign-off** — silence read as agreement; the reservation returns as a change request.
