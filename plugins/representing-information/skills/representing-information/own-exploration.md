# Your Own Exploration

The occasion defined by an absence: there is no audience. You are author, reader and narrator at
once, which collapses three of the four variables into trivial values — dwell is unbounded, author
presence total, control of the view complete — because the context every other occasion must build
into the artefact already sits in your head. What remains is the next action, and here it is to
**notice something**: to see a shape you were not expecting and follow it. That removes every
constraint except speed. This occasion punishes polish, which feels like work, produces a visible
improvement, and requires no thought about what the data is saying. Capable analysts run careful
statistics and then spend the afternoon on a colour scale nobody will ever see.

## The recipe

1. **Optimise for count, not quality.** Twenty ugly plots in ten minutes beats three beautiful ones;
   the surprise comes from coverage, and every minute spent making one presentable is a minute not
   spent making the next.
2. **Accept the defaults.** No titles, no chosen colours, no adjusted axes.
3. **Vary systematically** — every grouping, every pairing, several scales. The view you did not
   think to make is usually where the finding is.
4. **Fix legibility, never appearance.** Overplotting, a scale that crushes everything below the top
   value, a category order that defeats comparison: worth the minute, because the change lets *you*
   see something. A change that would only matter to a reader, when there is no reader, is
   procrastination.
5. **Note what you looked at**, so you know later whether "I tried one thing" or "I tried forty" —
   different evidentiary situations, and you will not remember which one you were in.
6. **Declare the boundary out loud.** The moment a plot is about to be shown to anyone, say it needs
   re-cutting *before* it is pasted, not after.

**The licence covers presentation, not inference.** The plot may be ugly, unlabelled and half-baked;
what it entitles you to believe is governed by exactly the same rules as everywhere else. A pattern
found across forty views is a hypothesis, and the number of views is precisely why. The informality
of the picture does not soften the error.

## Required moves

- [ ] **Volume over finish** — plot count is the metric.
- [ ] **Defaults left alone** unless the change reveals something to you.
- [ ] **Systematic variation**, not three plots and a conclusion.
- [ ] **Search path recorded.**
- [ ] **Inference kept honest** — exploratory findings reported as hypotheses.
- [ ] **Re-cut declared** the moment the plot leaves your screen.

## Failure modes

- **Polishing too early** — styling a plot one person will look at once. It feels productive and
  produces nothing. Building tooling instead of looking is the same failure, more expensive.
- **Shipping the exploratory plot unchanged** — the origin of most cross-occasion damage: default
  styling tolerated in a report because the reader has dwell and control, and then the report figure
  is sitting there when someone needs a slide. See `report-figure.md`, `live-talk.md` and
  `tool-recutting.md`.
- **Stopping at three plots** — treating the first interesting view as the finding.
- **Treating the exploratory result as confirmatory** — forty views, one pattern, reported as though
  it were the only thing tried.
- **Forgetting the search path** — not knowing later how much looking produced the pattern.
