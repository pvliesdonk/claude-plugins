# The Report Figure

The most generous reading conditions and the least help available. The reader controls the pace
entirely — minutes rather than seconds, re-readable, printable — but **nobody is present to
explain**, and the part people forget is that the figure is met **out of order**: skimming figures
before the text is standard behaviour, so the figure is routinely the first thing encountered, and
the next action is to verify, cite, or decide whether the paper is worth reading. The contract that
follows: **density is permitted; ambiguity is not.** A reader with time can decode a dense display;
no amount of dwell resolves an encoding nobody defined. Capable authors get the encoding and the
statistics right, then skip the caption's real workload — n, the uncertainty definition, every
panel and mark — because they already know what the panels are.

Self-containment here is an explicit editorial norm (journals ask that a reader understand the
figure without the main text) rather than an experimental result — but it is convention enforced by
people who have watched thousands of figures fail.

## The recipe

1. **Write the caption first.** If you cannot write it, the figure has no message yet — it is a data
   dump with axes.
2. **Make the caption carry the load**: what the figure shows in one sentence (an assertion is
   allowed and usually better than a description), every encoding defined, *n* and what it counts,
   and what the uncertainty interval actually is. "Error bars show variability" is not a definition
   and lets the reader import whichever one flatters you.
3. **Define every encoding inside the artefact**, preferring direct labels on the marks to a legend
   carried across the page.
4. **Show the distribution** wherever the claim depends on its shape, rather than a summary that
   could be hiding it.
5. **Run the covered-text test** on someone who did not do the work: cover the body text, and ask a
   competent stranger what the figure claims *and* how confident to be. Both halves matter.
6. **Design for greyscale, print and a bad screen** — and do not trust your tool's defaults, which
   were tuned for the occasion where the author is the only reader.

## Required moves

- [ ] **Caption written first**, and carrying interpretation rather than a label.
- [ ] **n stated**, with its unit of observation.
- [ ] **Uncertainty defined in words** — interval type and what it means.
- [ ] **Every panel, colour, shape and dashed line explained** inside the figure.
- [ ] **Distribution shown** where the claim rests on it.
- [ ] **Covered-text test run** on a stranger, not on yourself.

## Failure modes

- **The figure that needs the body text** — the characteristic failure, diagnosed in one move.
- **Legible only to its author** — panel letters explained three pages away, an unlabelled second
  axis, a caption reading "Results of the experiment."
- **The exploratory plot, shipped** — default styling, default limits, default colours. See
  `own-exploration.md`.
- **The undefined error bar** — load-bearing and never specified, so overlap means nothing.
- **The summary that hides the shape** — bars and means where the distribution was the finding.
- **Density mistaken for a licence to be unclear** — control of the view buys decoding time, not
  mind-reading.
