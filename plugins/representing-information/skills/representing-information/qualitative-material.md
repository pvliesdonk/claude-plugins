# Interviews, Themes and Free Text

Qualitative material resists the move that makes other representations easy: you cannot size it. And
here **the display is not a report of the analysis — it is where the analysis happens.** Condensation,
display and conclusion-drawing run concurrently, with an uncomfortable corollary: *you know what you
display*, and what never entered a cell never entered the argument. So draw it early, on partial
data; its first job is to show what you have not collected. (Theory with thirty years' standing, from
Miles and Huberman; no trial shows analysts working from matrices conclude better than those in prose.)

## The recipe

1. **Write the decision rules before the first cell, and publish them beside the display.** What
   defines a **row** — case, participant, event, document? If two rows are not the same kind of thing,
   comparison down the column is void. What defines a **column**, and did it come from the interview
   guide, the coding frame, or an emerging construct? **What may enter a cell** — verbatim,
   paraphrase, rating or presence mark, one register per column, since mixed registers are how a
   matrix quietly starts implying measurement. **Who entered it, from what.** And **what a blank
   means**: absent, not asked, not coded and declined are four facts that look identical, so give them
   distinct visible states. If you cannot say what a blank means, you are not ready to draw.
2. **Name the ordering principle in the title** — time-ordered, role-ordered, explanatory — because
   the arrangement is itself the claim. If a column keeps collapsing into its neighbour, the format
   was a hypothesis about the material and it has failed.
3. **Declare thinking instrument or evidence.** Displays are strong for thinking in any tradition and
   much weaker as evidence in constructionist, narrative and discursive work, where the warrant lives
   in the extended extract with its sequence intact. That objection — condensation into cells destroys
   the phenomenon — is serious and unresolved; preserve it rather than picking a side. And a display
   reads as a claim that the same frame was applied to every case; if it was improvised for some, do
   not draw it.
4. **Put each theme in a sentence with a verb before drawing anything.** A theme's warrant is not
   frequency: keyness is whether it answers the research question, so a pattern mentioned once by the
   only person positioned to notice it can outrank one mentioned by everybody — which may just be the
   interview guide talking. Themes are constructed, not found, so a layout that looks discovered lies
   about where the work happened. "Participants talked about training" is a **domain summary**.
5. **Never size a theme by extract count.** A thematic map may encode relations you argue for —
   subsumes, is in tension with, precedes, conditions — and never counts or magnitude: no node sized
   by mentions, no line weight for frequency, no left-to-right order for importance, no overlap for
   co-occurrence. Test: **if a reader could read your map as a Venn diagram or a bar chart, redraw
   it.** Ambiguous geometry resolves into the quantitative grammar readers already know. Label every
   link, or they supply a relation you never claimed.
6. **Treat counting as contested, not forbidden.** The prohibition is a reflexive-TA commitment
   following from a particular view of what a theme is, not a universal rule — in codebook TA,
   framework analysis and content analysis counting is standard, so naming your tradition settles most
   of the argument. The counter-case is serious too: numbers are already pervasive ("some", "most",
   "recurrent" are quantity claims), the real error is *concealed* counting, and quasi-statistics
   guard you against cherry-picking your own favourites. **Count only when all three hold:**
   **internal** to this corpus, claiming nothing about a population; a **fixed opportunity set**, so
   everyone counted was asked and silence is interpretable; and it **disciplines you** rather than
   persuading the reader. Report numerator and denominator with the opportunity set named ("7 of the
   14 asked about handover"); never a percentage, never on an axis, never fused to a theme's name.
7. **For free text, name the job: invitation, gist or evidence** — only the first two are open to
   bag-of-words forms. The case against the word cloud is usually made badly: font size does dominate
   recall, and size judgments are biased by word length and letter shape, but that study found the
   bias *smaller* than folklore claims, so overstating it loses the argument. The stronger objection is
   not perceptual and no redesign fixes it — a cloud destroys **syntax**; **negation** ("not safe"
   becomes *safe*, inverting the finding); **collocation** ("customer service" splits into two apparent
   concerns); **speaker**, so one voluble respondent is indistinguishable from thirty brief ones; and
   it hides **the stoplist and stemmer**, undisclosed decisions that change the output. Never use one
   to support an inference; as a declared invitation it is legitimate. Prefer concordance lines —
   words in context is the default, not the advanced option — and offer the replacement before
   attacking the cloud.
8. **Never present a topic-model topic as a theme.** A topic is a weighted list of co-occurring terms;
   a theme is an argued construction, and the gap is a human reading that must be visible. Optimising
   the standard selection statistic can make topics *less* interpretable — held-out likelihood is often
   negatively correlated with human judgments. A topic shown at all carries exemplar documents, the
   value of *k* noted as chosen rather than found, and the fact that another seed yields other topics.
9. **Attach the selection story to every quote.** An extract illustrates a claim the analysis
   established; it is rarely the evidence for one. Each carries an **ID** (so a reader can see six of
   eight quotes came from two people — tally your speakers before finalising), a **selection story**
   (most articulate instance of a pattern coded in N sources / a deviant case / the only
   counter-instance / chosen for clarity), and a **typical-or-extreme marker**, stated not implied.
   For every major claim show **one disconfirming case at equal prominence** — same type, same
   position, not a footnote. The chain claim → code → extract → source must be walkable both ways and
   exist before anyone asks; provenance reconstructed later is provenance invented. Personas and
   journey maps fuse evidence with invention, so mark each element evidenced or inferred *inside* the
   artefact, with a provenance strip and an expiry date.
10. **Pair strands, don't convert them.** A joint display earns its name only through an explicit
    **integration column** on every row: **confirmation** (with what each strand independently
    supports, so the agreement is not circular), **expansion** (in which direction), or **discordance**
    (and what the disagreement *means*). Discordance is the row people delete and the one worth most —
    high satisfaction beside interviews full of workarounds is a finding about what the item measured,
    not a data problem to reconcile. Both strands in native units and comparable space, built during
    analysis: a display assembled at write-up can only agree with conclusions already written around.
11. **Have five answers ready for "but how many?"** The asker is not innumerate; they have a decision
    and one heuristic for sizing risk. (a) **Answer the real question** — "how confident should I be
    acting on this?" — with the structure of the evidence: independent sources, consistency, the
    disconfirming case, fit with known mechanisms. (b) **Give the honest count** where the three
    conditions hold; withholding one looks evasive. (c) **Pair, don't convert.** (d) **Substitute
    severity for frequency** — "three of fourteen hit this, and all three lost data". (e) **Concede
    and propose the instrument**, when prevalence really is the decision-relevant quantity.

## Required moves

Capable analysts write careful prose and skip these. Check each:

- [ ] **Decision rules written first and published beside the display** — especially what a blank means.
- [ ] **Thinking instrument or evidence**, declared in the caption.
- [ ] **Every theme a sentence with a verb** someone could disagree with.
- [ ] **Geometry audited for smuggled quantity** — size, weight, order, overlap, distance each meaningless-by-design or explained.
- [ ] **Tradition named**; any count meeting all three conditions, with numerator, denominator and opportunity set.
- [ ] **Stoplist, stemming and blank rate disclosed**; contribution distribution checked first.
- [ ] **Selection story and ID on every extract**, plus one disconfirming case at equal prominence.
- [ ] **Integration verdict on every joint-display row**, discordance written in full.
- [ ] **The "how many" answer rehearsed beforehand**, so the move is not chosen under pressure.

## Failure modes

- **The write-up display** — built last from conclusions already fixed in prose; the tell is no empty cells and no surprises.
- **The undifferentiated blank** — a reader infers disagreement from a gap in the fieldwork. Its cousin: a clean grid implying an exhaustive corpus that was opportunistic.
- **The interview guide with arrows** — domain summaries drawn as structure, recapitulating the question list and calling it a finding.
- **The count-sized map** — bigger bubble, stronger theme: the most legible thing on the page and the least defensible.
- **The percentage that escapes** — a disciplining count lifted onto a slide, then into a chart, where it reads as prevalence.
- **The cloud as executive summary of open text** — tokenisation artefacts presented as findings at the moment of decision.
- **The labelled topic** — an analyst's interpretation sitting where model output appears to be.
- **The pull-quote slide** — one uncited quote doing the work of proof, usually from the most fluent participant: the qualitative equivalent of a truncated axis.
- **The decorative joint display** — two columns side by side with nothing said about their relationship. Adjacency is not integration.
- **Capitulation** — the demanded percentage chart with a caveat in small type. The chart travels, the caveat does not, and once a finding is in quantitative grammar the grammar wins.
- **Refusal** — principled, and the decision then gets made on whatever number someone else supplies.
